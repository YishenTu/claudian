# Authority-transfer constraints

- Closing a modal cannot interrupt an admitted hosting move; its remaining steps belong to the durable application operation.
- Production LAN/Cloud authority movement must not reuse physical Host-transfer packages. Reuse the existing lifecycle/persistence owners, never another catalog, index, or phase engine.
- Bind runtime resources to the exact operation and source/target generations, with no second transport cache in entry/recovery dispatch. Release owned connections with that binding; explicit caller-provided connections remain borrowed.
- Acquire the lifecycle arbiter before quiescence and during recovery. Consult `../lifecycle/AGENTS.md` for coexistence policy; local flags cannot bypass another durable owner.
- Any active LAN Member may propose a target, but only the admitted local Host installation can accept/quiesce it. Proposal alone cannot open Cloud connections or suspend work. Presence is neither consent nor identity proof; all-online participation is not required.
- Keep requester components and physical responsibility in separately atomic files. Synchronized installations cannot rewrite each other's components; foreign observations do not grant local lifecycle admission.
- Persist the exact successor before handing off predecessor ownership. A lost proposal response can adopt only its exact matching source observation; never reconstruct a different operation.
- Derive bounded child idempotency keys with operation-tagged digests. Appending suffixes to a valid maximum-length caller ID can make the operation unexecutable.
- Before cutover, retain the complete raw claim batch at source and acknowledge its exact digest at target. Custody acknowledgement is not redemption. Scrub source claims only after exact target-signed receipts or expiry.
- Imported Members stay unbound except the proved source/target Host. Claims bind existing identities; they do not authenticate Cloud ingress, mint membership, choose roles, or move refs. LAN claimants persist their credential before sending only its hash.
- Different-device Cloud-to-LAN Manager intent and its claimant belong to the initiating installation, including cleanup after later role changes. Foreign synchronization cannot perform those effects or block target physical recovery. Persist predecessor identity in the claimant so delivery order does not change ownership.
- Manager-reissued claims are distinct from source-issued claims. Never fabricate original-source status/receipt steps. Persist target success before local convergence; expiry cannot undo durable redemption, while ambiguous expiry needs exact authoritative binding/transfer evidence.
- Returning Cloud-to-LAN import may remove a locally owned former LAN authority only under the exact durable target operation, after validating the Cloud checkpoint and matching Cloud membership. Its Project must match, its generation must be older than the authenticated Cloud source, and it must not be serving. Intermediate Hosts may have run on other devices; adjacency is not required. Remove it through the installation owner before creating a provisional import; preserve the working copy and fail closed on foreign, same-generation, or newer authority state.
- Target preparation persists installation ownership before listener/TLS/staging effects. Cloud-to-LAN imports stay provisional until source relinquishment is proved. Published endpoint/proof cannot be withdrawn after possible acceptance without proved cancellation or forward recovery.
- Source relinquishment is irreversible and advances target generation exactly once. Cancellation before that boundary must prove the target did not accept the fence; neither timeout nor missing response is proof.
- Semantic checkpoints exclude physical database bytes, credentials, CA keys, working trees, unpublished/local-only work, and caches. Artifact creation needs durable operation ownership, atomic promotion, and exact cleanup; no absolute paths or sensitive content in journals/diagnostics.
- Preserve accepted source/target URLs inside frozen requests and signed proofs. They are historical locators, not listener ownership: address recovery retains the same installation trust and exact Project/generation/operation. Verify a discovered transfer endpoint before credentials; never rewrite proof or intent to change location.
- Journal begin/cancel before transport and retain exact requests after ambiguous outcomes. Local cancellation requires an unsent request or authoritative absence; persist physical reopening before terminal cancellation. A begun LAN-to-Cloud cancellation must durably record target cleanup and exact source reopening before sending/replaying the source-reopen acknowledgement. A stale-phase rejection may refresh a still-cancellable phase, never turn cancellation into resuming the move.
- Initialize a Cloud Vault credential only before a fresh begin or claim intent is persisted. Claimant journals pin the exact Cloud principal before target redemption; replay requires that principal even if a valid replacement credential is synchronized. Missing credentials cannot authorize a new principal during recovery.
- Recovery after redemption, membership convergence, or responder expiry must resolve only still-required authorities. Completed source/target convergence must work offline from durable proof; never require the relinquished authority to be reachable.
- Release active transfer/preparation ownership only after durable local convergence; retain exact replay identity, proofs, and claim obligations. Historical responder failures must not block current authority recovery. Empty claim batches may stop serving immediately. Before physical Host package capture, settle committed claim receipts under the claim-binding queue and captured resource incarnation. After a physical offer, old responders may replay receipts but cannot bind new credentials.
- Target expiry removes serving routes and retains endpoint ownership until cleanup succeeds. Run finalization outside the Host queue to avoid lock inversion, and retain shutdown ownership of in-flight cleanup.
